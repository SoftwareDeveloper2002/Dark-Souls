DARK SOULS (MOBILE) - GAME DESIGN DOCUMENT, SECTIONS 3 TO 9
Engine: Godot 4.7.2 | Target: Android (arm64) | Version 1.12.0 (code 23)

NOTE: Sections 1 and 2 (not covered here) are the concept / story overview. Everything below describes the game as it is built.


======================================================================
3. CONTROLS AND CAMERA
======================================================================

3.1 Input map

Touch (phone):
  Left half of screen ........ virtual stick: move
  Right half of screen ....... drag: look / rotate camera (used mainly in the labyrinth)
  Buttons .................... ATTACK, ROLL (dodge), BLOCK, ESTUS (heal), SKILL 1, SKILL 2,
                               SWAP (weapon), TORCH (labyrinth only), COMMAND (labyrinth only,
                               after a civilian is rescued), MAP, PAUSE

Keyboard (PC testing):
  Move with WASD / arrows, Q swap weapon, T torch, F command civilians, Z / C look left / right.
  Attack, roll, block, estus, skills and pause have their own keys (listed on the title screen's help page).

3.2 Camera
  - Open world: top-down / three-quarter chase camera that follows the hero.
  - Labyrinth: lower third-person camera behind the hero, FOV widens from 45 to 68 so the very
    high walls feel oppressive. The hero always faces where the camera looks.
  - Walls block the camera: it is pushed in front of a wall instead of clipping through it.
  - Trees between camera and hero become see-through (opacity fade) instead of the camera moving.
  - In the labyrinth the stick strafes relative to the camera; dragging the right side turns it.

3.3 Accessibility and readability
  - Large touch buttons with labels and icons; the COMMAND button shows STAY or FOLLOW.
  - Banners and floating text announce every important event (quest start, torch dropped, fail).
  - Voice lines for enemies, bosses, civilians and announcements, so the game is playable by sound too.
  - Health / stamina bars, boss bar, civilian health bars drawn on top of the world.
  - Settings: music on/off, sound effects on/off, FPS counter, graphics level, frame-rate cap.
  - Darkness in the labyrinth is strong but the torch light keeps the near area readable.


======================================================================
4. LEVEL DESIGN
======================================================================

4.1 World layout
  - One large forest map generated on Terrain3D terrain, with trees drawn as MultiMesh chunks.
  - A clickable map screen shows quest sites. Sites are placed along a route that leads from the
    start toward the final locations; each quest gets a site and an arena.
  - Landmarks: camps, ruins, emblem hiding places, the princess's prison, and the labyrinth.

4.2 Quest / arena structure
  - Each quest flattens a circular arena at its site; enemies spawn in a camp, then the quest is
    complete when the objective is done (kill all, kill the boss, find emblems, rescue, etc.).
  - Boundaries: the arena edge is marked by the forest and by collision; enemies do not leave it.
  - Spawn points: enemy camps are placed around the arena centre at a safe distance from the hero.

4.3 The labyrinth (quest 27)
  - 15 x 15 cell maze, 6 m cells, 7.5 m high walls, generated with a recursive backtracker
    (fixed seed, so every player gets the same maze).
  - Entrance and exit gates, 7 chests, 14 wall torches, civilians hidden in dead-end pockets,
    13 monsters spread away from the civilians.
  - Route: enter -> explore in the dark -> rescue every civilian -> lead them to the far gate.
  - Rationale: the maze is the "horror" level of the game; darkness, high walls and a third-person
    camera create tension that the open world does not have.

4.4 Rationale
  The game moves from open, readable combat (learn the weapons and skills) to constrained
  encounters (boss, maze) so difficulty comes from the space as well as from enemy stats.


======================================================================
5. CHARACTERS AND ABILITIES
======================================================================

5.1 The hero
  - Stats: health, stamina, level and experience, strength-style damage scaling from weapon,
    healing flask (Estus) with limited charges, rage meter that builds from taking and dealing hits.
  - Actions: light attack combo, roll with brief invulnerability, block (costs stamina),
    two weapon skills, weapon swap, execution (see 6.1).
  - Stamina limits attack, roll and block, which creates the risk / reward rhythm of the genre.

5.2 Weapons
  - Several weapon types (sword and others). Each weapon has its own two skills and its own
    fatality (finishing move). Skills have cooldowns and use rage.
  - Example skills: quake (ground slam), dash strike, flurry, fire shell (ranged).

5.3 Enemies
  - Small enemies: zombies, ghouls, hounds, skeletons, fiends, imps. They use real 3D bodies
    (Quaternius models) animated by a shared rig.
  - Ranged enemies fire projectiles that can also hurt civilians.
  - Bosses and a titan: more health, trash talk (voice lines), unique death animation and music.
  - Behaviour: approach, attack with a wind-up (readable telegraph), retreat / circle at low health.
    The enemy count and how far enemies are active depend on the graphics level.

5.4 Civilians (labyrinth)
  - 80 health each; hidden until the hero reaches them; once rescued they FOLLOW the hero's trail.
  - The player can command them to STAY or FOLLOW.
  - Monsters attack them. If any civilian dies the quest fails and the labyrinth resets.

5.5 Balancing choices
  - Enemy damage and health scale with quest number; rewards (experience, items) grow with it.
  - Cooldowns and rage cost keep skills strong but not spammable.
  - The torch rule (no weapon while holding it) forces a decision during every fight.


======================================================================
6. GAME SYSTEMS
======================================================================

6.1 Combat and damage
  - Hit detection by distance / arc around attacks; damage reduced by block; stamina on block.
  - Execution: grabbing a weakened enemy triggers a weapon-dependent fatality (heart rip,
    decapitation, uppercut, neck snap, slam, impale, shoot, stab, blast) with its own camera and
    player animation and announcer voice.

6.2 AI
  - Simple state machines per enemy: idle -> chase -> attack wind-up -> attack -> recover.
  - Civilians: hiding, follow, stay, escape, dead, gone.
  - Monsters inside the labyrinth also target civilians.

6.3 Objectives and progression
  - 27+ quests with different objectives: kill camps, bosses, find hidden emblems, rescue the
    princess, cross the labyrinth. Completing quests gives experience, level ups and items.
  - Story ending: after the princess is rescued, a comic-style epilogue plays (panels rendered
    with a comic shader: ink outline, posterised colour, halftone).

6.4 UI states
  - Title, play, pause, map, settings, quest banner, death / retry, epilogue.

6.5 Audio
  - Background music per situation (calm, combat, boss) plus ambience (rain, thunder, lurking sounds).
  - Voices for the hero hurt sounds, monsters, bosses (trash talk), civilians and announcements.

6.6 Pause, restart and save
  - Pause menu with settings. Death or quest failure lets the player retry the quest.
  - Progress is saved to a local savegame file (Continue on the title screen loads it).
    New Game deletes the old save.


======================================================================
7. ART AND AUDIO DIRECTION
======================================================================

7.1 Style
  - Dark, gritty, realistic fantasy (explicitly not cartoonish). Muted palette, cold moonlight,
    fog, warm orange firelight and torchlight as the only strong colour.
  - The comic epilogue is the deliberate exception: a graphic-novel look for storytelling.

7.2 Materials and lighting
  - Terrain3D with real ground textures; triplanar stone for labyrinth walls.
  - Moonlight (directional light), fog, rain and lightning flashes; point lights for torches
    and fires. In the labyrinth ambient light is almost removed so only torches light the maze.

7.3 VFX
  - Particles for fire, embers, hit sparks, blood, rain, ground impacts and skill effects.

7.4 Sound approach
  - Voice lines generated with the Windows speech engine and processed (pitch, low-pass,
    reverb) to sound menacing but intelligible; WAV files for short clips, compressed music.

7.5 Asset sources and credit plan (all free)
  - Character models, hair, and outfits: Quaternius packs (CC0).
  - Textures and terrain materials: CC0 sources (e.g. ambientCG / Poly Haven style libraries) and
    the Terrain3D demo assets.
  - Music and sound effects: free / CC0 packs downloaded for the project; licence files are kept
    beside the assets (for example LICENSE_Quaternius.txt).
  - Credits screen / documentation lists every pack with its author and licence.
  - Models were decimated and textures shrunk with Blender scripts (tools/prep_*.py) for phones.


======================================================================
8. IMPLEMENTATION MAPPING
======================================================================

8.1 Feature -> script (res://scripts/)
  Main game loop, combat, quests, camera ........ game.gd
  Autoload: quality, FPS, device info, audio .... settings.gd
  Autoload: music ............................... music.gd
  Touch controls and buttons .................... touch_controls.gd
  Walls, maze, chests, torches, collision ....... structures.gd
  Fatalities / executions ....................... execution.gd
  Rescued civilians ............................. civilians.gd
  Labyrinth torch ............................... torch.gd
  Character bodies and rig posing ............... models.gd
  Trees (MultiMesh chunks, see-through) ......... forest.gd
  Screen effects (fog, darkness, rain) .......... fx.gd
  Voices and sounds ............................. vox.gd
  Quest texts ................................... quests.gd
  Title and help ................................ title.gd
  Comic epilogue ................................ comic.gd, comic_art.gd, comic.gdshader

8.2 Pseudocode - dynamic resolution (performance)
  every 0.6 s:
      fps = frames / time
      if fps < 88% of target:  scale = max(0.62, scale - 0.08)
      elif fps > 97% of target: scale = min(1.0, scale + 0.04)
      apply render scale (rate-limited: 1.2 s after lowering, 3 s after raising)
  if still slow at the smallest scale for ~8 s: lower the graphics level by one.

8.3 Pseudocode - labyrinth quest
  start quest 27:
      flatten ground (a few hundred points per frame)
      build walls -> chests -> torches (yielding between steps)
      when finished: spawn civilians + monsters
  each frame:
      if a civilian is hit to zero health: quest failed -> reset maze after 3.6 s
      if all rescued and all at the gate: quest complete

8.4 State diagram (hero)
  idle -> move / attack / roll / block / skill / execute / hurt -> idle; health 0 -> dead -> retry.
  Torch: held (no weapon) <-> dropped (can fight).

8.5 Changes from the original concept
  - Fatalities and skills now depend on the weapon (originally one shared set).
  - The labyrinth was made larger, darker, with civilians to protect (originally empty).
  - Camera: trees fade instead of pushing the camera; walls rotate / push the camera.
  - Maze generation moved to a step-by-step build and a spatial grid to avoid lag on phones.


======================================================================
9. TECHNICAL SPECIFICATIONS
======================================================================

  Engine ............ Godot 4.7.2 (stable), GDScript
  Renderer .......... GL Compatibility (OpenGL ES 3.0), for wide phone support
  Terrain ........... Terrain3D addon
  Target platform ... Android, arm64-v8a only, signed release APK (no Gradle build)
  Test device ....... Honor X9b (8 GB RAM); must also run on 4 GB phones
  Package ........... app category: game; isGame = true (Android Game Mode aware)
  Build settings .... export preset "Android Release", version 1.12.0 / code 23,
                      output build/dark-souls-release.apk
  Graphics levels ... 6 (VERY LOW to REALISTIC); default chosen by RAM on first run
                      (<= 4.6 GB: level 1, <= 6.6 GB: level 2, else level 3)
  Frame-rate cap .... 30 / 45 / 60 / 90 / 120 / unlimited (default 60); forced to 30 in
                      Android Battery Game Mode, battery saver or when the phone is hot
  Performance target. 60 fps on 8 GB phones, a stable 30+ fps on 4 GB phones
  Performance tools . dynamic resolution, auto-lower graphics, light limit by level, MultiMesh
                      forest, pooled characters, spatial grid for maze collision, async maze build
  Device detection .. Android Game Mode (standard / performance / battery), thermal status,
                      battery saver, screen refresh rate, GPU name - shown in Settings

  Known limitations
  - Not yet measured on a phone after the latest optimisation pass; real-device frame times
    are the next thing to verify.
  - Some figures are still built when first needed, which can cause a small hitch the first time.
  - The Honor Game Space may need the game added manually to show it as a game.
  - Voices are synthesised, not recorded by actors.
